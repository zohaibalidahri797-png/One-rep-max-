Questions & Answers

Everything you need to know regarding 1RM estimation accuracy, formula selection, repetition fatigue, and plate math.

A One Rep Max (1RM) is the maximum amount of weight an individual can lift for a single, full-range repetition of a given exercise with proper technique. It represents your absolute peak muscular strength and serves as the baseline for percentage-based training cycles.

When calculated from sets of 2 to 6 repetitions performed to near-failure (RPE 9-10), 1RM equations have a high accuracy rate with under 2% margin of error. As reps exceed 8 to 10, cardiovascular conditioning, anaerobic lactic threshold, and local endurance introduce higher statistical variance.

Boyd Epley is the most widely validated for lower-body compound lifts (Squat, Deadlift). Jerry Mayhew et al. is mathematically optimized for horizontal pressing (Bench Press) using exponential decay. Matt Brzycki is ideal for strict overhead presses and conservative estimations.

True 1RM testing places immense acute stress on the central nervous system (CNS), joints, and connective tissue, requiring days of specialized recovery. Submaximal calculation from a heavy 3-rep or 5-rep set provides equivalent programming data without disrupting your training rhythm.
